## v015 Its or it's?
Series: English Made Easy · all classes · 23.1 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
The dog wagged it's tail. Spot the mistake? It's, with an apostrophe, means it is, or it has. The apostrophe stands in for missing letters. Its, without one, means belonging to it. Like his and her, no apostrophe. Quick test: say it is. If the sentence still makes sense, write it's. The dog wagged it is tail? No. So it's its tail. Read it's as it is, every time.

Caption:
Fun fact: around 1600, people really did write the possessive as "it's" — "April with it's sweet showers." The apostrophe-free "its" only won in the 1700s, once "it's" took over from the old contraction "'tis" ✍️
Today the rule is simple: if you can't swap in "it is" or "it has", drop the apostrophe.
Try it: "The school changed (its / it's) timings." Answer in the comments.

## v016 Does studying all night work?
Series: Study Smart · all classes · 25.0 s · 70 words

Voiceover:
Studying all night before an exam feels productive. Does it stick? That's cramming. It feels like learning, but it often fades fast. Now split the same hours across a few days. Spread-out study is remembered longer, even with the same total time. A big review rated spacing one of the two best study techniques. Try two short sessions this week, not one long night. Spread it out, and it stays.

Caption:
Cramming feels faster because everything is fresh in your head — but that feeling of “I know this” often doesn't last 📚 Learning feels slower when it's spread out, yet it stays longer.
The other top-rated technique in the same review? Testing yourself (see our earlier reel on it). Rereading and highlighting were rated low.
Simple plan: one short session a few days before the test, and one the evening before.

## v017 Why does a cold glass 'sweat'?
Series: Science Around Us · all classes (states of matter / condensation) · 25.2 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Pour ice-cold water into a steel glass. Soon the outside is wet. Leaking? No. That water was never inside the glass. The air around us holds water vapour that we cannot see. The cold glass cools the air touching it. Cooled vapour turns into tiny liquid drops. This is condensation. That's why it's so common on humid monsoon days. The water came from the air, not from the glass.

Caption:
Proof it's not leaking: fill the same glass with water at room temperature and the outside stays dry 🥛 Only the cold one "sweats".
Same science, different places: specs fogging when you step out of an AC room, drops on the outside of an AC window, and dew on grass in winter mornings.

## v018 Why does a torch app want your contacts?
Series: Smart & Safe · all classes, parents · 24.0 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
A torch app asks to read your contacts. Why would a torch need that? It doesn't. Contacts have nothing to do with light. Mumbai cyber police have warned about apps asking for permissions they don't need. Many of us just tap Allow without reading. Before you allow, ask: does this app need it? If not, tap Don't allow. You can change it anytime in Settings. Your data, your permission.

Caption:
Two-minute phone check-up 📱 On Android: Settings › Apps › pick an app › Permissions. Or open the Permission manager to see every app that can use your location, camera or microphone.
For location, camera and mic you can often choose "Only while using the app" or "Ask every time" instead of always.
Do this check-up with your parents too — it takes two minutes per phone.

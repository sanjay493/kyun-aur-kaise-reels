## v071 Charged more than the MRP?
Series: Money Smart · all classes, parents · 27.4 s · 65 words

Voiceover:
A chips packet marked twenty rupees. The shopkeeper asks for twenty five. Allowed? M.R.P. means Maximum Retail Price. The highest price it can be sold for. India's packaged goods rules say no shop can sell a packed item above its M.R.P. Below M.R.P.? That's fine. That's a discount. Overcharged? Call the National Consumer Helpline, one nine one five. Check before you pay. Maximum means maximum.

Caption:
The rule is Rule 18 of the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011: no shop, wholesaler or even the maker can sell a packed item for more than the retail sale price printed on it 🛒
Your state's Legal Metrology department enforces it, and the National Consumer Helpline takes complaints in 17 languages.
Ways to complain: call 1915 (toll-free), SMS or WhatsApp 8800001915, the NCH app, the UMANG app, or consumerhelpline.gov.in. Keep the bill, or a photo of the pack and the price you were charged.
Selling below MRP is allowed, so it's worth comparing shops too.
#MoneySmart #ConsumerRights #KnowYourRights #FinancialLiteracy #SmartShopping

Sources: Department of Consumer Affairs, Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 441 (answered 24 Jul 2024; "Rule 18 of the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 provides that no retail dealer or other person including manufacturer, packer, importer and wholesale dealer shall make any sale of any commodity in packed form at a price exceeding the retail sale price thereof"; "State Governments are empowered to take action for selling packaged commodities at the price higher than MRP"; grievances "in 17 languages ... through a toll-free number 1915"; channels "Whats App, SMS, mail, NCH app, web portal, Umang app") https://fcainfoweb.nic.in/PMS/writereaddata/2024_LS_B_441.pdf ;

## v072 Why does a rubbed refill pick up paper?
Series: Science Around Us · Class 8 (Some Natural Phenomena: charging by rubbing) · 24.6 s · 65 words

Voiceover:
Rub a pen refill with polythene. Now it picks up bits of paper! How? Rubbing gives it a small electric charge, called a static charge. Rub two balloons with wool, and they push apart. Like charges repel. A charged balloon pulls a charged refill. Unlike charges attract. Hair standing up when you pull off a sweater? Static charge again. Like charges push. Unlike charges pull.

Caption:
Try NCERT's activity at home: rub a used ballpen refill hard with a piece of polythene and hold it near tiny bits of paper. Then try a plastic comb run through dry hair ⚡
Static charges don't move by themselves, which is why the refill keeps its charge for a little while.
Your Class 8 book also links this to lightning: charges build up in clouds, and when they meet you get a giant spark.
Works best on a dry day. Can you guess why it's harder in the monsoon?

## v073 Who's or whose?
Series: English Made Easy · all classes · 22.5 s · 70 words

Voiceover:
Whose bag is this? Or who's bag? Only one is right. Who's, with an apostrophe, is short for who is, or who has. Who's coming? Who's eaten my laddoo? Whose means belonging to whom. Whose bag is this? Test: swap in who is. Who is bag is this? Makes no sense. So, whose. Like his, her and its, whose has no apostrophe. Can you say who is? Who's. Can't? Whose.

Caption:
Whose works for things too, not just people: "the book whose cover is torn" ✍️
Same pattern as it's (it is) and its (belonging to it): the apostrophe marks missing letters, not ownership.
Try these: ___ turn is it to bat? ___ going to the canteen? I know a girl ___ sister plays for India. ___ finished the homework? (Answers: Whose, Who's, whose, Who's)
#EnglishMadeEasy #EnglishGrammar #CBSEEnglish #LearnEnglish #GrammarTips

Sources: Merriam-Webster, "'Who's' vs. 'Whose'" ("Who's is a contraction of who is or who has"; whose is "a possessive adjective meaning 'of or relating to whom or which'"; "the book whose cover is torn"; possessive adjectives "don't contain an apostrophe: my, your, his, her, its, our, their") https://www.merriam-webster.com/grammar/whos-whose-difference-usage-pronouns ;
Grammar Monster, "Who's or Whose?" ("If you cannot substitute the 'who's' in your sentence with either 'who is' or 'who has,' then it is wrong!") https://www.grammar-monster.com/easily_confused/whos_whose.htm ;

## v074 Phone stolen? Block it
Series: Smart & Safe · all classes, parents · 23.9 s · 66 words

Voiceover:
Your phone is stolen. Scared someone will misuse it? You can block it. Use the government's Sanchar Saathi portal or app. You'll need a police complaint copy, your phone's I.M.E.I. number, and a mobile number for the O.T.P. Then it's blocked on all Indian networks, and police can trace it. Found it later? Unblock it on the same portal. Lost phone? Report it, then block it.

Caption:
Sanchar Saathi is run by the Department of Telecommunications. By December 2025 it had helped block over 42 lakh lost or stolen phones, and 7.23 lakh were returned to their owners 📱
Before you file: get a new SIM for your old number (you'll need it for the OTP) and keep the police complaint copy ready. Save the 18-digit request ID you get after filing; you need it to unblock the phone.
Buying a second-hand phone? Check it isn't reported stolen: SMS KYM followed by the IMEI to 14422.
Share this with your family.
#SmartAndSafe #CyberSafety #DigitalSafety #SancharSaathi #PhoneSafety

## v039 Fewer laddoos or less laddoos?
Series: English Made Easy · Class 6 (countable and uncountable nouns) · 23.0 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Fewer laddoos or less laddoos? Most people say less. Here's the rule. If you can count it, one, two, three, use fewer. Fewer laddoos, fewer mistakes. If you can't count it, like water, sugar or time, use less. Less water, less time. Quick test: can you put a number in front? Three laddoos, yes. Three milks, no. So: fewer exams, less homework. Count it? Fewer. Can't count it? Less.

Caption:
Same idea with "many" and "much": how many laddoos? How much milk? Countable nouns go with fewer and many; uncountable nouns go with less and much 🍬
Try these: ___ students came today. There's ___ traffic on Sundays. I made ___ spelling mistakes this time. (Answers: fewer, less, fewer)
Bonus: "I have fewer books than you" but "I earn less money than him". Money is uncountable, but rupees are countable: fewer rupees, less money.
#EnglishMadeEasy #EnglishGrammar #CBSEEnglish #LearnEnglish #GrammarTips

Sources: Oxford Learner's Dictionaries, Grammar: "Quantifiers: more, less and fewer" ("We use fewer with countable nouns when we compare two things, to say that one thing is smaller in number than another thing"; "We use less with uncountable nouns ... smaller in amount"; examples "I have fewer books than you." / "I earn less money than him.") https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/grammar/online-grammar/quantifiers-more-less-and-fewer

## v040 Why does an iron gate rust?
Series: Science Around Us · Class 7 (Physical and Chemical Changes) · 22.5 s · 64 words

Voiceover:
Why does an old iron gate turn reddish brown? That's rust. It's not iron any more. It's a new substance, iron oxide. Iron rusts only when it gets both oxygen and water. Humid air rusts it faster. Near the sea, salty water makes it even faster. So we paint gates, grease chains, or coat iron with zinc. Stop air and water, stop the rust.

Caption:
Rusting is a chemical change: a new substance forms. Iron + oxygen + water → rust (iron oxide) 🔩
Coating iron with a layer of zinc is called galvanisation.
Try it (NCERT activity): put iron nails in three bottles: plain tap water, water boiled for several minutes, and water with a film of cooking oil on top. Check them after a few days. Which nails rust the most?

Sources: NCERT Class 7 Science, Chapter "Physical and Chemical Changes" ("For rusting, the presence of both oxygen and water (or water vapour) is essential"; "if the content of moisture in air is high ... rusting becomes faster"; "The salt water makes the process of rust formation faster"; "One simple way is to apply a coat of paint or grease"; "This process of depositing a layer of zinc on iron is called galvanisation"; "Rust is not iron"; Iron + Oxygen + water → rust (iron oxide); Extended Learning Activity with three bottles of nails) via IIT Kanpur SATHEE https://sathee.iitk.ac.in/ncert-books/class-07/science/chapter-05-physical-and-chemical-changes ;

## v041 Is that really your uncle calling?
Series: Smart & Safe · all classes, parents, grandparents · 24.5 s · 70 words

Voiceover:
Your uncle calls from a new number, crying. There's been an accident. Send money now! But scammers can now copy a voice with A.I., using clips from social media videos. They create panic so you don't stop to think. So hang up. Call back on the number you've saved for him. Families can also agree on a secret code word for emergencies. A familiar voice isn't proof. Call back first.

Caption:
Fraudsters can take a voice sample from social media videos, or even from a short "wrong number" call, and use AI to copy it. Then they fake an emergency and ask for quick payment, often by UPI 📞
Caller ID can be faked too, so always call back on the number you already have saved.
Pick a family code word tonight, one that isn't on anyone's social media. Share this with your parents and grandparents.
If money has already been sent, call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in straight away, and inform your bank.

## v042 ₹100 at 10% for 3 years: ₹130?
Series: Money Smart · Class 8 (Comparing Quantities) · 25.6 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
One hundred rupees at ten percent a year. After three years, is it one hundred thirty? Simple interest says yes: ten rupees every year. Compound interest adds interest to interest. Year two: ten percent of one hundred ten is eleven. Year three: twelve rupees ten paise. Total: one hundred thirty three rupees ten paise. The longer you wait, the bigger the gap. Interest on interest. Time does the work.

Caption:
Simple interest: the principal stays ₹100 every year. Compound interest: each year's interest is worked out on the previous year's amount, so the principal keeps growing 📈
The formula from your Class 8 book: A = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ. Check it: 100 × 1.1 × 1.1 × 1.1 = 133.1
Over 3 years the gap is small (₹3.10). Over 20 years at 10%, simple interest gives ₹300, compound gives about ₹673.
The same rule works against you on loans and credit-card dues: interest on unpaid interest grows fast too.
#MoneySmart #FinancialLiteracy #CompoundInterest #Class8Maths #CBSEMaths

Sources: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics, Chapter "Comparing Quantities" ("The interest is calculated on the amount of the previous year. This is known as interest compounded or Compound Interest (C.I.)"; "the Principal remains the same under Simple Interest, while it changes year after year under compound interest"; table for ₹100 at 10%: interest after 3 years ₹30 simple vs ₹33.10 compound; A = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ) via IIT Kanpur SATHEE https://sathee.iitk.ac.in/ncert-books/class-08/mathematics/chapter-7-comparing-quantities ;
20-year figures computed: 100 + 20 × 10 = 300; 100 × 1.1^20 ≈ 672.75.

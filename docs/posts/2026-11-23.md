## v063 Guess first, then learn
Series: Study Smart · all classes · 25.2 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Before reading a chapter, try its questions. Even if you get them wrong. In one experiment, college students read an essay about vision. Some first tried test questions on it. They got almost all of them wrong. Others just spent that time studying. On the final test, the guessers did better: seventy five percent versus fifty six. The key: check the right answers straight after. Guess first. Then learn.

Caption:
Researchers call this the pretesting effect: trying to answer a question before you've learnt the material, then seeing the right answer, helps you remember it better than spending the same time just reading 📖
In the study, students got 95% of the pretest questions wrong, and still remembered more later. Getting it wrong is fine, as long as you check the correct answer soon after.
Try it tonight: before reading the next chapter, turn its exercise questions or headings into questions and write a quick guess for each. Then read and check.
#StudySmart #StudyTips #ExamPreparation #CBSE #BoardExams

Sources: Richland, L. E., Kornell, N. & Kao, L. S. (2009), "The pretesting effect: Do unsuccessful retrieval attempts enhance learning?", Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied 15(3), 243–257 ("Participants in 5 experiments read an essay about vision"; Experiment 1: "participants answered 5% of the questions correctly" on the pretest; "testing resulted in better posttest performance (M = 75%...) than did the provision of extra time to study the same material (M = 56%...)"; "Although participants largely failed on the initial test (answering 95% of the questions incorrectly), the effect of those failures was to increase retention") https://learninglab.uchicago.edu/Pre-Testing_files/RichlandKornellKao.pdf ;
Scientific American Mind, "The Pluses of Getting It Wrong" ("students who make an unsuccessful attempt to answer a test question before receiving the correct answer remember the material better than if they simply study the information"; "Getting the answer wrong is a great way to learn—as long as you receive the correct answer shortly afterward") https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-pluses-of-getting-it-wrong/

## v064 Practice or practise?
Series: English Made Easy · all classes · 26.6 s · 70 words

Voiceover:
Cricket practice or cricket practise? Both are real words. In British English, practice with a C is the noun, like cricket practice. Practise with an S is the verb. I practise every day. Same pattern as advice and advise. Advice is the noun, advise is the verb. Easy trick: ice is a noun. Advice and practice are nouns. American English uses practice for both. A thing? C. Doing it? S.

Caption:
Quick test: can you put "a" or "the" in front of it? Then it's a noun, spelt with a C: the practice, some advice. Is someone doing it? Then it's a verb, spelt with an S: I practise, she advises ✍️
Watch the -ing and -ed forms too: practising, practised (British spelling).
Try these: We have hockey ___ after school. You need to ___ your tables. My teacher gave me good ___. Can you ___ me? (Answers: practice, practise, advice, advise)
#EnglishMadeEasy #EnglishGrammar #CBSEEnglish #LearnEnglish #SpellingTips

Sources: Dictionary.com, "Practice vs. Practise" (15 Aug 2022; "In British English and other varieties, the spelling practise is used as a verb and the spelling practice is used as a noun"; "American English uses practice as both the noun and verb form"; "advise is used as the verb ... while advice is the noun") https://www.dictionary.com/articles/practice-vs-practise

## v065 "Update KYC or your account will be blocked"?
Series: Smart & Safe · all classes, parents, grandparents · 24.7 s · 66 words

Voiceover:
S.M.S.: Your bank account will be blocked today. Update K.Y.C. now. Click this link. The Reserve Bank has warned about this fraud. Scammers create false urgency and threaten to freeze your account, so you panic. The link may install an app or ask for your O.T.P., PIN or password. Don't click. Call your bank's official number, or visit your branch. Panic message? Pause. Ask your bank.

Caption:
The RBI has warned that fraudsters send calls, SMS and emails about "KYC updation", threaten that your account will be frozen, blocked or closed, and send links that install unverified apps 🏦
Never share your OTP, PIN, password or card details, and don't send copies of KYC documents to unknown people.
Take the bank's phone number only from its official website, or ask at your branch how to update KYC.
Already lost money? Call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in, and tell your bank straight away. Share this with your parents and grandparents.
#SmartAndSafe #CyberSafety #ScamAlert #KYCFraud #DigitalSafety

Sources: Reserve Bank of India, press release 2023-2024/1794, "RBI cautions against frauds in the name of KYC updation" (2 Feb 2024; "unsolicited communications, including phone calls/SMS/emails"; "installing unauthorized or unverified apps through links provided in the messages"; "creating a false urgency and threatening of account freezing/blocking/closure"; "Do not share account login credentials, card information, PINs, passwords, OTPs with anyone"; "Do not share KYC documents or copies of KYC documents with unknown or unidentified individuals or organizations"; "directly contact their bank/ financial institution"; bank contact number "only through its official website/ sources"; "Enquire with their bank branch"; National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal and helpline 1930) https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=57244

## v066 35 × 35 in your head?
Series: Maths Without Fear · Class 8 (Squares and square roots) · 25.5 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Square thirty five in your head? Try this. Take the first digit, three. Multiply by the next number, four. Twelve. Then put twenty five after it: one thousand two hundred twenty five. Why? It's thirty times forty, plus five times five: twelve hundred plus twenty five. Try eighty five: eight times nine is seventy two. Seventy two twenty five. Ends in five? Times the next number, then twenty five.

Caption:
Why it always works: a number ending in 5 is 10n + 5, and (10n + 5)² = 100 × n(n + 1) + 25. So you multiply n by the next number and write 25 after it 🧮
It works for bigger numbers too: 115² → 11 × 12 = 132, so 13225.
Try these: 25², 65², 95², 105². (Answers: 625, 4225, 9025, 11025)
#MathsWithoutFear #CBSEMaths #Class8Maths #MathsTricks #MentalMaths

Sources: Harvey Mudd College Math Fun Facts, "Squares Ending in 5" ("if the first digit is N and the second digit is 5, then the last 2 digits of the answer will be 25, and the preceding digits will be N*(N+1)"; "multiply (10N+5)(10N+5) and interpret!"; 115² = 13225 "because 11*12 = 132") https://math.hmc.edu/funfacts/squares-ending-in-5/ ;
Arithmetic checked: 35² = 1225 = 30 × 40 + 25; 85² = 7225; 25² = 625; 65² = 4225; 95² = 9025; 105² = 11025; 115² = 13225.

## v011 Why does cutting an onion make you cry?
Series: Science Around Us · all classes · 23.7 s · 70 words

Voiceover:
Why does cutting an onion make you cry, but holding one doesn't? Inside the onion, certain chemicals and an enzyme are kept apart. Your knife breaks the cells. They mix and form a new chemical that floats into the air. When it reaches your eyes, it irritates the nerves there. So your eyes make tears to wash it away. A sharp knife sprays less. Tears are your eyes protecting themselves.

Caption:
The onion keeps its "tear gas" ingredients in separate compartments until your knife breaks them open 🧅 The chemical that reaches your eyes has a name chemists love: syn-propanethial-S-oxide.
Kitchen test from a 2025 study: a sharp knife and slow, gentle cuts released fewer tear-causing droplets. Surprise: chilled onions released more, not fewer.

## v012 Did the whole class really notice?
Series: Teen Talk · all classes · 24.0 s · 68 words

Voiceover:
Spilled chai on your shirt? Feels like the whole class noticed. Did they? Psychologists call this the spotlight effect. In one study, students wore an embarrassing T-shirt into a room. They guessed about half the people noticed it. Only about a quarter actually did. Most people are busy thinking about their own day. So next time, relax a little. Most people notice your slips less than you think.

Caption:
That replay in your head of the moment you tripped on the stairs? You're probably the only one still watching it 🔦
In the study, students guessed about 50% of the room could identify the person on their T-shirt. About 25% actually could.
Next time you feel everyone is staring, ask: "Would I even remember this if someone else did it?"

## v013 Why is 6 ÷ ½ not 3?
Series: Maths Without Fear · CBSE Class 7 (Working with Fractions, dividing by a fraction) · 24.8 s · 69 words spoken

Voiceover:
Six divided by a half is not three. It's twelve. How? Division asks: how many of these fit inside that? Take six rotis. Cut each roti into halves. Each roti gives two halves. So six rotis give twelve halves. That's why dividing by a half is the same as multiplying by two. Same with a third: six divided by one third is eighteen. Smaller pieces, more of them fit.

Caption:
"Divide by half" and "divide into half" are two different questions 🫓 6 ÷ 2 = 3 (split into 2 parts), but 6 ÷ ½ = 12 (count the halves).
That's the reason behind the "flip and multiply" rule: 6 ÷ ½ = 6 × 2.
Try it: how many quarter-rotis are in 5 rotis?

## v014 Can police arrest you on a video call?
Series: Smart & Safe · all classes, parents · 23.6 s · 66 words spoken

Voiceover:
A 'police officer' on a video call says you're under digital arrest. Real? No. There is no such thing as a digital arrest in Indian law. Scammers pose as police, C.B.I. or customs officers and keep you on camera. They scare you, then demand money. Real officers never arrest anyone on video calls. Cut the call, tell an adult, report it on one nine three zero.

Caption:
Fake uniforms, fake "police station" backgrounds, fake documents — all on a video call 📵 The aim is to keep you scared and on camera until you pay.
Share this with the elders at home too; they are often the ones targeted.
Report cyber fraud on the national helpline 1930 or at cybercrime.gov.in.

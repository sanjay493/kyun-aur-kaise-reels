## v019 Is 50% + 20% off really 70% off?
Series: Maths Without Fear · Class 8 (Comparing Quantities: discounts) · 26.1 s · 58 words

Voiceover:
A shop says fifty percent off, plus an extra twenty percent off. Is that seventy percent off? Let's check with a shirt that costs one thousand rupees. Fifty percent off brings it to five hundred. Now the extra twenty percent is taken from five hundred, not one thousand. That's only one hundred off. You pay four hundred. That's sixty percent off, not seventy. Discounts on discounts multiply. They don't add up.

Caption:
Shortcut for any "extra off" sale 🛍️ Turn each discount into what you still pay: 50% off → you pay 0.5, 20% off → you pay 0.8. Multiply: 0.5 × 0.8 = 0.4, so you pay 40% and save 60%.
Bonus: the order doesn't matter. 20% off first, then 50%, still lands on ₹400.
Try it: 40% off + extra 10% off on ₹2000. How much do you pay? Answer in the comments.
#MathsWithoutFear #Percentages #CBSEMaths #MathsTricks #IndianStudents

Sources: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics, Chapter 7 "Comparing Quantities" (discounts) https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/hemh107.pdf ;
arithmetic checked: 1000 × 0.5 = 500; 500 × 0.8 = 400; (1000 − 400)/1000 = 60%.

## v020 Why can't you sleep early anymore?
Series: Teen Talk · all classes, parents · 24.6 s · 70 words

Voiceover:
Wide awake at eleven at night, but a zombie at seven in the morning? During puberty, your body clock shifts about two hours later. Melatonin, the sleepy hormone, is released later at night. But school starts early, and teens need eight to ten hours of sleep. Bright screens at night keep your brain awake. So dim the screens well before bed. You're not lazy. Your body clock just runs late.

Caption:
Parents, this one's for you too 🌙 A late body clock is normal biology in the teen years, not laziness or bad discipline.
What helps: a regular sleep schedule, and screens off well before bed. The American Thoracic Society suggests switching off phones, TV and games two hours before bedtime.
Under 13? The recommendation is 9 to 12 hours.

## v021 Why do stars twinkle?
Series: Science Around Us · Class 10 (The Human Eye and the Colourful World: atmospheric refraction) · 24.6 s · 70 words

Voiceover:
On a clear night, stars twinkle. Is the star flickering? Stars are so far away, each looks like a tiny point. That light passes through moving air to reach your eye. Warm and cool air bend the light differently, and keep shifting. So the starlight wobbles, brighter, then dimmer. Near the horizon, light crosses more air, so stars twinkle more. The twinkle isn't in the star. It's in our air.

Caption:
Board-exam bonus ⭐ Why don't planets twinkle? They are much closer, so they look like tiny discs, not points. The flicker from different parts of the disc averages out, and the planet shines steadily.
Tonight's test: find the brightest steady "star" in the evening sky. If it doesn't twinkle, it's probably a planet.

## v022 Googled a helpline number? Careful.
Series: Smart & Safe · all classes, parents · 26.7 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Refund stuck, so you search online for the helpline number? Careful. The R.B.I. warns these numbers are often fakes put up by fraudsters. The fake agent sounds helpful, then asks you to install a screen-sharing app or share an O.T.P. Real banks never ask for your O.T.P., PIN or password. Take the number only from the official website or app. Don't trust the search result. Trust the official source.

Caption:
Fraudsters make fake numbers look official in search results, so the top number isn't always the real one 📞 Your bank's number is usually printed on the back of your debit card, and it's in the bank's official app.
Already shared something? Call your bank to block the card or account, then report it at 1930 (National Cyber Crime Helpline) or cybercrime.gov.in.
Share this with the family member who handles online shopping refunds.
#SmartAndSafe #CyberSafety #OnlineFraud #DigitalSafety #IndianParents

## v059 8% of 50? Flip it!
Series: Maths Without Fear · Class 7–8 (Percentages, Comparing Quantities) · 22.1 s · 65 words

Voiceover:
Quick: eight percent of fifty? Hard? Flip it. It's the same as fifty percent of eight. Half of eight: four. Why? Percent means out of a hundred. Both are eight times fifty, divided by a hundred. And you can multiply in any order. Same answer. Try four percent of twenty five. Flip it: twenty five percent of four. One! Stuck on a percentage? Flip it.

Caption:
x% of y is always the same as y% of x, because both are x × y ÷ 100. Pick whichever way round is easier in your head 🧮
Useful flips: 25% means a quarter, 50% means half, 10% means move the decimal point one place.
Try these: 16% of 25? 12% of 50? 18% of 5? (Answers: 25% of 16 = 4; 50% of 12 = 6; 5% of 18 = 0.9)
#MathsWithoutFear #CBSEMaths #Percentages #MathsTricks #MentalMaths

Sources: Mathnasium (Upland), "Mathnasium #MathTricks: Percents (Commutative Property)" (15 Feb 2022; "48% of 50" rearranged with "the commutative property of multiplication" to "50% of 48", which is 24) https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/upland/news/mathnasium-mathtricks-percents-commutative-property-1381255411 ;
Arithmetic checked: 8 × 50 ÷ 100 = 4; 50 × 8 ÷ 100 = 4; 4 × 25 ÷ 100 = 1; 16 × 25 ÷ 100 = 4; 12 × 50 ÷ 100 = 6; 18 × 5 ÷ 100 = 0.9.

## v060 Why does a haldi stain turn red with soap?
Series: Science Around Us · Class 7 (Acids, Bases and Salts / Exploring Substances: Acidic, Basic, and Neutral) · 24.1 s · 70 words

Voiceover:
A haldi stain on your white shirt. Wash it with soap, and it turns red! Why? Turmeric is a natural indicator. Its colour shows what kind of substance it touched. Soap is a base. And bases turn turmeric red. Lemon juice is an acid, but turmeric stays yellow, just like in water. So turmeric spots bases, but can't tell an acid from water. Haldi turned red? A base is there.

Caption:
Your Class 7 book lists turmeric, litmus and china rose (gudhal) petals as natural indicators 🌼
Make turmeric paper at home: mix a little haldi with water to make a paste, spread it on blotting or filter paper and let it dry. Cut it into strips.
Now test with an adult's help: soap solution, lemon juice, plain water, baking soda in water. Which ones turn the strip red?
#ScienceAroundUs #CBSEScience #Class7Science #KitchenScience #NCERT

Sources: NCERT Class 7 Science (older edition), Chapter "Acids, Bases and Salts" ("Turmeric, litmus, China rose petals (Gudhal), etc., are some of the naturally occurring indicators"; "a turmeric stain on my white shirt is turned to red when it is washed with soap. It is because the soap solution is basic"; soap listed among bases) via IIT Kanpur SATHEE https://sathee.iitk.ac.in/ncert-books/class-07/science/chapter-04-acids-bases-and-salts ;
Teachoo, NCERT Curiosity Class 7 Science, Chapter 2 "Exploring Substances: Acidic, Basic, and Neutral", 2.1.3 Turmeric as an Indicator ("Soap solution turns turmeric paper red"; "Turmeric paper cannot differentiate between acidic and neutral substances"; "Lemon juice and water both keep turmeric paper yellow") https://www.teachoo.com/31276/6416/2.1.3-Turmeric-as-an-Indicator/category/Concepts/

## v061 Why does your voice crack?
Series: Teen Talk · Class 8 (Reaching the Age of Adolescence) · 25.5 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Your voice suddenly squeaks mid-sentence. What just happened? At puberty, your voice box, the larynx, starts to grow. In boys it grows bigger, showing as the Adam's apple. Voices get deeper. While it grows, its muscles sometimes lose control. The voice cracks or turns hoarse. This lasts days or weeks, then settles. Many boys go through it, at different ages. Not a flaw. Just a voice box growing up.

Caption:
Your Class 8 science book explains it: at puberty the voice box (larynx) grows, and in boys it grows larger, which you can see as the Adam's apple. Girls' voice boxes are smaller, so it is hardly visible 🎙️
Puberty starts at different ages for different people, so some voices change earlier and some later. Both are normal.
If a friend's voice cracks in class, don't make it a joke. It's just growing up, and it happens to lots of people.
#TeenTalk #Puberty #GrowingUp #Class8Science #Parenting

Sources: NCERT Class 8 Science, Chapter "Reaching the Age of Adolescence" ("At puberty, the voice box or the larynx begins to grow. Boys develop larger voice boxes"; "The growing voice box in boys can be seen as a protruding part of the throat called Adam's apple"; "In girls, the larynx is hardly visible from the outside because of its small size"; "sometimes, the muscles of the growing voice box go out of control and the voice becomes hoarse"; "This state may remain for a few days or weeks after which the voice becomes normal"; "the period of adolescence varies from person to person"; "These changes are a natural part of growing up") via IIT Kanpur SATHEE https://sathee.iitk.ac.in/ncert-books/class-08/science/chapter-07-reaching-the-age-of-andolescence

## v062 "This ₹10 coin is fake"? Not so fast
Series: Money Smart · all classes, parents · 25.3 s · 70 words

Voiceover:
Shopkeeper: this ten rupee coin is fake, I won't take it. Is he right? Ten rupee coins come in many designs. By twenty eighteen, fourteen. New themes keep coming, and coins last years, so old and new circulate together. The Reserve Bank says all are legal tender, and can be accepted for payments. Banks were told to accept them too. New design isn't fake. Ten rupees is still ten rupees.

Caption:
"Legal tender" means money that the law says can be used to pay. The RBI says every ₹10 coin design it has issued is legal tender 🪙
Different designs show different themes (economic, social and cultural), and because coins last a long time, older and newer designs are used at the same time.
The RBI has also advised banks to accept ₹10 coins for transactions and exchange at all their branches.
#MoneySmart #FinancialLiteracy #RBI #KnowYourMoney #IndianCurrency

Sources: Reserve Bank of India, press release 2017-2018/1950, "RBI reiterates legal tender status of ₹ 10 coins of different designs" (17 Jan 2018; "reluctance on part of traders and members of public to accept ₹ 10 coins due to suspicion about their genuineness"; "So far the Reserve Bank has issued ₹ 10 coins in 14 designs"; "distinctive features to reflect various themes of economic, social and cultural values"; "As coins have longer life, coins of different designs and shapes circulate in the market at the same time"; "All these coins are legal tender and can be accepted for transactions"; "advised banks to accept coins for transactions and exchange at all their branches") https://rbi.org.in/commonman/english/scripts/PressReleases.aspx?Id=2499

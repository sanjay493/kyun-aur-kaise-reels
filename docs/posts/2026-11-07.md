## v031 Studying till 3 a.m. before the exam?
Series: Study Smart · all classes, parents · 23.8 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Studying till three a.m. the night before the exam? Your brain may need that sleep more. Sleep isn't a break from learning. It's part of it. During sleep, the brain replays what you learned and strengthens those memories. Skip sleep, and new learning gets harder the next day. One sleep scientist puts it simply: you can't pull an all-nighter and still learn effectively. So study, then sleep on it.

Caption:
The night before an exam, the last hour of sleep may help more than the last hour of revision 😴
An NIH article quotes researchers saying that without sleep, your ability to learn new things could drop by up to 40%.
Try it this exam season: finish revising an hour before bed, then do a quick 5-minute self-test in the morning instead of reading everything again.
Parents: a calm, early night before a paper is part of exam prep, not time lost.

## v032 "I am living here since 2020" — spot the mistake
Series: English Made Easy · Class 6–10 · 21.0 s · 63 words

Voiceover:
I am living in Pune since twenty twenty. Spot the mistake? It started in the past and is still true, so use the present perfect. I have lived in Pune since twenty twenty. Since gives the starting point. Since Monday, since twenty twenty. For measures the length of time. For three days, for six years. Since is the start. For is how long.

Caption:
A quick test: if you can say "from ___ till now", use SINCE. If you can say "for how many ___?", use FOR ✍️
Same rule with other verbs: "I have known her since Class 3." "We have been friends for five years." "She has had this phone since Diwali."
Try it: "My dadi has lived in Lucknow (since / for) forty years." Answer in the comments.
#EnglishMadeEasy #EnglishGrammar #CBSEEnglish #CommonMistakes #SpokenEnglish

Sources: Oxford Learner's Dictionaries, "Present perfect simple with for and since" ("We use the present perfect to talk about present activities that started in the past"; "We use for to talk about the period of time up to the present"; "We use since to talk about the time when an activity started"; "She has lived here for over 30 years.") https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/grammar/online-grammar/present-perfect-simple-with-for-and-since

## v033 Why does a straw look broken in water?
Series: Science Around Us · Class 8 and 10 (light, refraction) · 21.9 s · 68 words

Voiceover:
Put a straw in a glass of nimbu paani. Why does it look broken? The straw isn't bent. The light coming from it is. Light bends when it crosses from water into air. That's called refraction. Your eyes and brain assume light came in a straight line. So they place the underwater part of the straw somewhere it isn't. The straw stays straight. Its light takes a turn.

Caption:
Try it now: put a pencil in a steel glass of water and look from the side, then from straight above. The "break" changes as you move your head 🥤
Same trick of light: a coin at the bottom of a bucket, or a fish in a pond, is not quite where it looks. Light bends at the water's surface, and your brain is fooled.
Coming up in Class 10: this same bending is how lenses in spectacles work.
#ScienceAroundUs #Refraction #CBSEScience #PhysicsFun #IndianStudents

Sources: The Physics Classroom, "The Broken Pencil" ("The position of the pencil under the water is shifted relative to the position of the pencil above the water"; "the explanation of this phenomenon involves the refraction of light") https://physicsclassroom.com/mmedia/optics/bp ;
The Physics Classroom, "Refraction and the ray model of light" ("when light passes from one medium into a second medium, the light path bends"; "The eye and brain assume that light travels in a straight line"; "the submerged portion of the pencil appears to be in a different location") https://physicsclassroom.com/class/refrn/u14l1b

## v034 "Electricity will be cut tonight" — real?
Series: Smart & Safe · all classes, parents · 24.6 s · 68 words

Voiceover:
Your electricity will be cut tonight at nine p.m. Call our officer now. Real? P.I.B. Fact Check called this message a scam. The Ministry of Power never sent it. Call, and the fake officer asks for bank details or a remote-access app. That app can hand them control of your phone. Check only through your electricity company's official website or app. Panic is their tool. Pause is yours.

Caption:
Signs it's fake ⚡ a deadline of "tonight", a personal mobile number for an "officer", and a reason like "last month's bill not updated".
Never install an app or share bank details because a caller asks. Look up the number yourself on your power company's official website or bill.
Teens: show this to the elders at home. These messages usually target parents and grandparents.
Lost money to a scam? Report it at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930.
#SmartAndSafe #ScamAlert #CyberSafety #FraudAwareness #DigitalSafety

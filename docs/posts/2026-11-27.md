## v079 Then or than?
Series: English Made Easy · all classes · 22.6 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Didi is taller then me, or taller than me? One letter makes the difference. Than, with an A, is for comparing. Taller than me. More than ₹50. Rather than. Then, with an E, is about time. First brush, then sleep. Also: if it rains, then take an umbrella. Tip: compare and than both have an A. When and then both end in -en. Comparing? Than. Time? Then.

Caption:
"Than" also follows other, less and rather: "other than milk", "less than a minute", "rather than walk" ✍️
"Then" also hides in phrases about time: back then, until then, now and then, just then.
Quick quiz, answer in the comments:
1. I'd rather walk ___ take the bus.
2. Finish your homework, ___ play.
3. Back ___, phones had buttons.
#EnglishMadeEasy #EnglishGrammar #LearnEnglish #CBSEEnglish #CommonMistakes

Sources: Merriam-Webster, "When to use 'then' and 'than'" ("Than is used in comparisons"; than is "the word that follows other, rather, less, and more"; "Then is used to talk about time"; "back then", "just then", after "since" and "until"; "It's also used in if-then statements: If it rains, then you will need to use your umbrella"; "they both end in -en", of when and then) https://www.merriam-webster.com/grammar/when-to-use-then-and-than

## v080 Why is a pinhole camera's image upside down?
Series: Science Around Us · Class 7 (Curiosity Ch. 11 "Light: Shadows and Reflections", 11.7 Pinhole Camera) · 23.3 s · 68 words

Voiceover:
Point a pinhole camera at a candle. The flame appears upside down. Why? Because light travels in straight lines. Light from the flame's top goes straight through the tiny hole, to the screen's bottom. Light from the bottom goes up to the top. So the whole picture flips. Make the hole too big, and the image turns blurry. Straight lines crossing at one hole: an upside down image.

Caption:
It flips left to right too, not just top to bottom 🕯️ Each point of the object can reach the screen along only one straight line through the hole.
Make one (NCERT Activity 11.10): take two cardboard boxes that slide into each other. Make a small hole in the middle of one face of the bigger box. In the smaller box, cut a 5–6 cm square and cover it with tracing paper. Slide it in, cover your head and the camera with a dark cloth, and look at a brightly lit distant object.
Slide the inner box closer to the hole and the image gets smaller. Never point it at the Sun.

Sources: NCERT, Curiosity, Textbook of Science for Grade 7, Chapter 11 "Light: Shadows and Reflections", 11.7 Pinhole Camera ("light rays from an object pass through a tiny hole (a pinhole) and form an image on a screen"; "The image of the candle flame is upside down, that is, inverted"; Activity 11.10 boxes, "5–6 cm" square, "thin translucent paper (like a tracing paper)", "Cover your head and the camera with a dark cloth"; Activity 11.2 "light travels in a straight line") https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/gecu111.pdf ;

## v081 Study as if you'll teach it
Series: Study Smart · all classes, parents, teachers · 25.4 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Want to remember a chapter better? Study it like you'll teach it. In a 2014 study, students read a passage. Some expected a test, others to teach it. Twist: nobody actually taught. Everyone took the test. Those expecting to teach remembered more, especially main points, and organised it better. Preparing to teach makes you find key points and order them. Study like a teacher, not just a student.

Caption:
The only thing that changed was what students expected before reading. Same passage, same test 📚
Try it tonight: before you open a chapter, tell yourself you'll explain it to a younger cousin or a friend tomorrow. While reading, note the 3 main points and the order you'd teach them in. Then close the book and explain it out loud.
Teachers and parents: "You'll teach this to the class tomorrow" costs nothing and changes how students read.
#StudySmart #StudyTips #ExamPreparation #LearningTips #StudentLife

Sources: Washington University in St. Louis, The Source, "Expecting to teach enhances learning, recall" (28 Jul 2014), on Nestojko, Bui, Kornell & Bjork, "Expecting to teach enhances learning and organization of knowledge in free recall of text passages", Memory & Cognition, 2014 (one group "told they will be tested", the other "led to believe they are preparing to teach the passage to another student"; "In reality, all participants were tested, and no one actually engaged in teaching"; "learners expecting to teach recalled more material correctly, they organized their recall more effectively and they had better memory for especially important information"; "When teachers prepare to teach, they tend to seek out key points and organize information into a coherent structure") https://source.washu.edu/2014/07/expecting-to-teach-enhances-learning-recall/

## v082 Look, tilt, touch: checking a ₹500 note
Series: Money Smart · all classes, parents · 25.3 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Got a ₹500 note? Here are three checks the RBI lists. One, look. Hold it against light, and Mahatma Gandhi's watermark appears. Two, tilt. The security thread changes from green to blue. So does the ₹500, with the rupee sign, at the bottom right. Three, touch. Five raised bleed lines on the left and right help visually impaired people. Look, tilt, touch. Every note, every time.

Caption:
More features RBI lists on the ₹500 note 🔍
• A see-through register and a latent image, both of the numeral 500.
• Tiny micro letters 'भारत' and 'India'.
• Number panel with digits growing from small to big.
• A circle with ₹500 in raised print on the right, and Mahatma Gandhi's portrait and the Ashoka pillar emblem in raised print.
• Back side: the Red Fort with the Indian flag, and the year of printing.
The note is stone grey and measures 66 mm × 150 mm.

## v007 Does rereading your chapter actually work?
Series: Study Smart (new) · all classes · 26.1 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Reading a chapter again and again feels like studying. But does it work? In a famous experiment, students learned a short passage. Some kept rereading. Others read once, then tested themselves. Five minutes later, the rereaders did better. But one week later, the self-testers remembered more. Pulling an answer out of memory makes it stronger. Rereading just feels familiar. So close the book and ask: what do I remember?

Caption:
Rereading feels productive because the page looks familiar 📖 Recall is what builds memory.
Try it tonight: read one section, close the book, write down everything you remember, then check what you missed.
Fun twist from the study: students who only reread were the most confident, and remembered the least a week later.
#StudySmart #StudyTips #BoardExams #CBSE #IndianStudents

Sources: Roediger & Karpicke (2006), "Test-enhanced learning", Psychological Science 17(3) https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x ;
Roediger & Karpicke (2018), "Reflections on the resurgence of interest in the testing effect", Perspectives on Psychological Science (describes the SSSS vs STTT experiment, 5-min vs 1-week results and students' predictions) https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691617718873

## v008 Why isn't 1 a prime number?
Series: Maths Without Fear · CBSE Class 6 (Prime Time / factors and multiples) · 23.6 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Two is prime. Three is prime. Why not one? A prime has exactly two factors: one and itself. Seven has two. But one has only one factor, so it fails. Deeper reason: every number splits into primes in only one way. Twelve is two times two times three. If one were prime, you could keep adding ones forever. So one stays out, and every number keeps one prime recipe.

Caption:
1 isn't left out by accident 🔢 Keeping it out means every number has exactly one prime factorisation, which is the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic you'll meet again in Class 10.
Quick check: 1 is neither prime nor composite. 2 is the only even prime.
#MathsWithoutFear #CBSEMaths #Class6Maths #PrimeNumbers #IndianStudents

Sources: Scientific American, Evelyn Lamb, "Why isn't 1 a prime number?" https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/roots-of-unity/why-isnt-1-a-prime-number/ ;
NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, "Real Numbers" (Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic).

## v009 Which password is really stronger?
Series: Smart & Safe · 26.0 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Which password is stronger? One full of symbols, or three random words? People swap letters for numbers and add a symbol at the end. Hackers know these tricks, so such passwords are easy to guess. Now try three random words: chai, kite, monsoon. Long, odd, and easy to remember. Length beats tricks. Britain's National Cyber Security Centre recommends it. Three random words, and a different set for every account.

Caption:
"Pa55word!" ticks every box on a sign-up form and is still easy to guess 🔐 Pick three random words only you would put together (not the ones in this video!).
Use a different set for every account, and turn on two-step verification for email and social media.

## v010 If sunlight is white, why is the sky blue?
Series: Science Around Us · CBSE Class 10 (The Human Eye and the Colourful World, scattering of light) · 22.3 s · 64 words

Voiceover:
Sunlight looks white. So why is the sky blue? White light is a mix of all the rainbow colours. Tiny air particles scatter blue light far more than red. So scattered blue reaches your eyes from every part of the sky. At sunset, light crosses more air. Blue gets scattered away, leaving orange and red. A blue sky is just sunlight, bouncing off air.

Caption:
Same sunlight, two colours: blue at noon, orange at sunset 🌅 It all depends on how much air the light travels through.
Board-exam keyword: this is scattering of light. Smaller particles scatter shorter wavelengths (the blue end) more.
#ScienceAroundUs #CBSEScience #Class10Science #ScatteringOfLight #IndianStudents

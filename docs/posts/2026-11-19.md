## v047 Paid to like videos? It's a trap
Series: Smart & Safe · all classes, parents · 22.6 s · 70 words

Voiceover:
A WhatsApp message: like a few videos, earn money from home. And they pay! That small payment is bait, to win your trust. Next, they ask you to pay for bigger prepaid tasks, promising more commission. You pay more. Then the money is stuck, and they vanish. Asked to pay before you earn? Stop. Report it on one nine three zero. Easy money that wants your money is a trap.

Caption:
The Ministry of Home Affairs has warned about "task-based" scams: liking YouTube videos, following Instagram accounts, rating hotels or writing reviews for money. The first few payments are real, so the "job" feels genuine 📵
Then come "prepaid tasks": you deposit money for a bigger commission, and soon your deposits are stuck and the "employer" disappears.
The simple rule: never send money to anyone who promises to pay you for easy online tasks.
Lost money? Call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in straight away.

## v048 Rain will affect or effect the match?
Series: English Made Easy · all classes · 22.8 s · 67 words

Voiceover:
Rain will affect the match. Or is it effect? Lots of people mix these two up. Affect is usually a verb. It's the action, to change something. Effect is usually a noun. It's the result of that change. Quick trick: A for action, affect. E for end result, effect. So, rain affects the match. And the effect? No match today. Affect does. Effect is what you get.

Caption:
Spot the verb: "Screen time can affect your sleep." Spot the noun: "Screen time has an effect on your sleep." If you can put "an" or "the" in front, you probably need effect ✍️
You'll also see "with immediate effect" in school notices and news: that's the noun again.
Try these: Exam stress can ___ your appetite. The new timetable comes into ___ on Monday. Did the rain ___ your plans? (Answers: affect, effect, affect)
#EnglishMadeEasy #EnglishGrammar #CBSEEnglish #LearnEnglish #GrammarTips

Sources: IDP IELTS, "Grammar 101: affect vs effect" ("Affect is usually a verb, and it means to impact or change"; "Effect, on the other hand, is usually a noun that you would use to indicate the result of a change"; example "He resigned with immediate effect.") https://ielts.idp.com/prepare/article-grammar-101-affect-vs-effect

## v049 1/3 roti + 1/4 roti = 2/7 roti?
Series: Maths Without Fear · Class 6 (Fractions, adding unlike fractions) · 24.3 s · 68 words

Voiceover:
One third of a roti, plus one quarter of a roti. Is that two sevenths? But two sevenths is smaller than one third. Adding can't make it smaller! The pieces are different sizes. So cut both rotis into twelve equal pieces. One third is four twelfths. One quarter is three twelfths. Four plus three: seven twelfths of a roti. Make the pieces the same size first. Then add.

Caption:
Why twelfths? 12 is the smallest number that both 3 and 4 divide into, so both fractions can be written with the same denominator: 1/3 = 4/12 and 1/4 = 3/12 🫓
Quick sense check for any sum: the answer must be bigger than each fraction you added. 7/12 is about 0.58, bigger than both 1/3 and 1/4.
Try these: 1/2 + 1/3 = ? and 3/4 + 1/3 = ? (Answers: 5/6 and 13/12, which is 1 1/12)
#MathsWithoutFear #CBSEMaths #Class6Maths #Fractions #NCERT

Sources: NCERT Ganita Prakash Class 6, Chapter 7 "Fractions", Addition of Fractions ("Unlike fractions must first be expressed using a common denominator"; Figure it out exercise includes 3/4 + 1/3) via Teachoo https://www.teachoo.com/subjects/cbse-maths/class-6/chapter-7-ganit-prakash/ ;
Cut the Knot, "Why 1/3 + 1/4 = 7/12?" (sharing apples: "each received exactly 7/12 of an apple"; "Fractions 4/12 and 3/12 correspond to the two steps") https://www.cut-the-knot.org/do_you_know/SevenTwelveth.shtml ;
Arithmetic checked: 2/7 ≈ 0.286 < 1/3 ≈ 0.333; 1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6; 3/4 + 1/3 = 13/12.

## v050 Buy 2 get 1 free: half price?
Series: Money Smart · Class 8 (Ganita Prakash II, Fractions in Disguise) · 23.7 s · 69 words

Voiceover:
Buy two, get one free. Sounds like half price, right? Each T-shirt costs one hundred rupees. You pay two hundred and take home three. So each one costs about sixty six rupees sixty seven paise. You saved one third, about thirty three percent. Not half. Buy one, get one free? That one really is fifty percent off. Divide what you pay by what you get. That's the real price.

Caption:
Your Class 8 maths book has this exact question: three shops sell the same item at the same price. Shop A: buy 1 get 1 free. Shop B: buy 2 get 1 free. Shop C: buy 3 get 1 free 🛍️
At ₹100 an item that works out to ₹50, about ₹66.67 and ₹75 per item, so 50%, about 33% and 25% off.
And a free item only saves you money if you actually needed it. If you need 4 items, Shop A costs ₹200 and the other two cost ₹300.
#MoneySmart #FinancialLiteracy #Class8Maths #CBSEMaths #SmartShopping

Sources: NCERT Ganita Prakash Class 8 Part 2, Chapter 1 "Fractions in Disguise", Figure it out (pages 28–30), Question 13 ("Three shops sell the same items at the same price"; Shop A "Buy 1 and get 1 free", Shop B "Buy 2 and get 1 free", Shop C "Buy 3 and get 1 free"; solution: effective price ₹50, ₹66.66…, ₹75; discount 50%, 33.33..%, 25%; for 4 items Shop A ₹200 vs ₹300) via Teachoo https://www.teachoo.com/27701/5842/Question-13/category/Figure-it-out---Page-28--29--30/ ;
Arithmetic checked: 200 ÷ 3 = 66.67; 100 ÷ 300 = 33.3%.

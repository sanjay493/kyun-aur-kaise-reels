# Educational Shorts Content Playbook
## Instagram Reels | YouTube Shorts | CBSE Classes 6–10

**Purpose:** Build an engaging educational channel for Indian teenagers, students, parents and teachers using short, curiosity-driven videos with simple 2D animations.

**Primary audience:**
- Students aged approximately 11–16.
- CBSE students in Classes 6–10.
- Parents and grandparents.
- Teachers and education enthusiasts.

**Content style:** Simple English, relatable Indian examples, surprising questions, short explanations and practical takeaways.

# PART 1 — INITIAL LIBRARY OF 25 VIDEO SCRIPTS

## CATEGORY A: MATHEMATICS

### 1. Why does anything raised to the power zero equal 1?

Why is any non-zero number raised to zero equal to 1?

Look at this pattern: 2³ = 8, 2² = 4, 2¹ = 2. Every time the power decreases by one, we divide by 2.

So, 2⁰ must be 2 divided by 2, which equals 1.

That's why any non-zero number raised to zero equals 1. Maths follows patterns!

### 2. Why is dividing by zero not allowed?

What happens if you divide 10 by zero?

Division asks how many equal groups you can make. If you divide 10 by 2, you get 5. But what would 10 divided by zero mean?

There is no number that you can multiply by zero to get 10.

That's why division by zero is undefined, not simply a difficult calculation.

### 3. Why does multiplying two negative numbers give a positive answer?

Why does minus multiplied by minus become plus?

Think of this pattern: 3 times minus 2 is minus 6. Two times minus 2 is minus 4. One times minus 2 is minus 2.

Each time the first number decreases by one, the answer increases by 2.

Following the pattern, minus 1 times minus 2 must equal positive 2!

### 4. Can you find 15% of a number without a calculator?

Can you calculate 15% of 200 in your head?

Here's a simple trick. First, find 10% of 200. That's 20. Next, find 5%, which is half of 10%, giving 10.

Now add both answers: 20 plus 10 equals 30.

So, 15% of 200 is 30. Break difficult calculations into easier parts!

### 5. Why do we use pi in circles?

Why does every circle have the same special number, pi?

Measure the circumference of a circle and divide it by its diameter.

Try it with a small coin and then a large plate. The measurements will give approximately the same ratio: 3.14.

This special number is called pi. It helps us calculate the circumference and area of circles!

### 6. How can you check a multiplication answer using another method?

Made a multiplication mistake in your exam? Here's a useful checking trick!

Take 23 times 12. The answer is 276.

Now add the digits of each number: 2 plus 3 gives 5, and 1 plus 2 gives 3. Multiply 5 by 3 to get 15, then add its digits to get 6.

The answer, 276, also gives 6. This is called the digit-sum check, though it cannot catch every mistake.

## CATEGORY B: SCIENCE

### 7. Why does ice float on water?

Why does ice float instead of sinking in water?

Most substances become denser when they turn solid. But water behaves differently.

When water freezes, its molecules arrange themselves into an open structure that takes up more space.

This makes ice less dense than liquid water. That's why ice floats, allowing aquatic life to survive beneath frozen surfaces!

### 8. Why does metal feel colder than wood?

Why does a metal spoon feel colder than a wooden spoon in the same room?

Surprisingly, both may be at exactly the same temperature!

Metal conducts heat much faster than wood. When you touch metal, heat leaves your hand more quickly.

Your skin senses that faster heat loss as coldness. So the difference is heat transfer, not necessarily temperature!

### 9. Why can't we see stars during the daytime?

Where do all the stars disappear during the daytime?

They haven't disappeared! Stars are still shining in space.

But sunlight enters Earth's atmosphere and gets scattered in different directions, making the sky appear bright blue.

The much brighter daytime sky hides the faint light from most stars. At night, the sky becomes dark, and we can see them more easily!

### 10. Why do we get hiccups?

Why does your body suddenly make a strange hic sound?

A hiccup happens when your diaphragm, the muscle that helps you breathe, contracts involuntarily.

Air rushes into your lungs, and your vocal cords quickly close, producing that familiar sound.

Hiccups are usually harmless and temporary. Your breathing muscle is simply having an unexpected spasm!

### 11. Why does a pressure cooker cook food faster?

Why does food cook faster inside a pressure cooker?

Normally, water boils at around 100 degrees Celsius at sea level.

Inside a pressure cooker, pressure increases, allowing water to reach a higher boiling temperature.

The hotter steam and water transfer heat to the food more quickly. That's why pressure cookers can save cooking time and energy!

## CATEGORY C: ENGLISH AND LANGUAGE

### 12. Why is the word QUEUE so strange in English?

Why does the word QUEUE have four letters waiting silently at the end?

The word is pronounced like the letter Q. The remaining letters don't represent separate spoken sounds here.

English spelling has developed through centuries of language changes, which is why spelling and pronunciation don't always match.

Remember: queue means a line of people waiting!

### 13. Why does English have silent letters?

Why do we write the K in KNIFE if we don't pronounce it?

English spelling preserves traces of older pronunciations and historical language changes.

Words such as knife, knee and know once had a pronounced K sound in earlier forms of English.

Pronunciation changed, but the spelling remained. That's why some English letters are silent today!

### 14. Can one punctuation mark change the meaning of a sentence?

Can one tiny comma completely change a sentence?

Compare these two sentences: Let's eat, Grandma! And let's eat Grandma!

In the first sentence, you're inviting Grandma to have food. In the second, the missing comma creates a completely different and frightening meaning!

Punctuation isn't decoration. It helps readers understand exactly what we mean.

## CATEGORY D: SOCIAL SCIENCE AND GENERAL KNOWLEDGE

### 15. Why does India have one official time?

Why does India have one official time even though it stretches so far from east to west?

The Sun rises earlier in eastern India than in western India.

But India uses one standard time based on the 82.5-degree east longitude.

This makes railway schedules, offices and national communication easier to coordinate. One country, one official time!

### 16. Why is the Indian Constitution called a living document?

How can a country's Constitution change without being completely rewritten?

India's Constitution includes a process for amendments.

As society changes, Parliament can amend certain constitutional provisions by following the required legal procedure.

This allows the Constitution to respond to new needs while preserving its basic framework. That's why it is often described as a living document!

### 17. Why do we celebrate Independence Day on 15 August?

Why does India celebrate Independence Day on 15 August?

On this date in 1947, India became independent from British rule.

But independence did not mean every challenge disappeared overnight. The country still had to build institutions, strengthen democracy and bring people together.

15 August reminds us not just of freedom, but also of the responsibility that comes with it!

## CATEGORY E: TEENAGE PSYCHOLOGY AND MORAL VALUES

### 18. Should you change yourself just to fit in with friends?

Would you do something wrong just because all your friends are doing it?

Imagine your friends laughing at someone and asking you to join them.

You might fear becoming the next target if you refuse. But real friendship should not require you to hurt someone or betray your values.

A simple sentence can help: "I'm not comfortable doing that." Your choices define you, not peer pressure!

### 19. Why is saying sorry not a sign of weakness?

Why do some people find it so difficult to say sorry?

Sometimes our ego makes us defend our mistakes instead of accepting them.

But a sincere apology shows that you understand how your actions affected someone.

Try saying, "I was wrong, and I will try not to repeat it."

Admitting a mistake doesn't make you smaller. It gives you a chance to grow!

### 20. Why should you never judge someone by their marks?

Does getting 95 marks make someone more intelligent than a student who gets 65?

Marks show performance in a particular examination. They don't measure every talent, skill, creative idea or personal quality.

One student may be excellent at mathematics, another at music, sports or communication.

Use marks to identify what needs improvement, not to decide someone's worth!

### 21. Why is listening more important than always giving advice?

When your friend is upset, should you immediately start giving advice?

Not always!

Sometimes people don't need solutions immediately. They need someone who listens without laughing, judging or interrupting.

Try asking, "Do you want my advice, or would you just like me to listen?"

A little understanding can make someone feel supported and less alone.

### 22. Why should teenagers spend time with grandparents?

What can your grandparents teach you that a textbook cannot?

They may share stories about their childhood, challenges they faced and how everyday life has changed.

These conversations can help teenagers understand family history, patience and different generations.

You don't always need an expensive gift to make someone happy. Sometimes, ten minutes of genuine conversation is enough to strengthen a family bond!

### 23. Why is comparing yourself with your classmates unfair?

Why do you feel unhappy when your friend scores more marks than you?

Because comparison makes you focus on someone else's progress instead of your own.

Everyone has different strengths, learning speeds and opportunities.

Instead of asking, "Why am I not like them?", ask, "What can I improve compared with last month?"

Your real competition is your previous self!

## CATEGORY F: STUDY HABITS AND TIME MANAGEMENT

### 24. Why is studying for 30 focused minutes sometimes better than sitting with books for three hours?

Can studying for 30 focused minutes be more useful than sitting with books for three hours?

It can be, especially when those three hours are filled with distractions.

Focused study means choosing one task, keeping your phone away and actively working on it.

Take short breaks when needed, then return with attention.

Remember, study quality matters alongside study time!

### 25. Why should you study a difficult subject before you feel completely ready?

Why do we keep postponing the subject we find most difficult?

Because difficult tasks can make us uncomfortable, so our brain looks for easier activities.

But waiting for motivation can waste valuable time.

Try studying just one small topic or solving three questions to get started.

Action often creates motivation. You don't need to finish the whole chapter today. Just begin!

# PART 2 — REUSABLE MASTER PROMPT FOR CLAUDE

Copy the following prompt into Claude whenever you want to generate a new video.

## Claude master prompt

You are an educational content researcher, short-form video scriptwriter and creative director specialising in Indian school students.

Your job is to generate original, interesting, curiosity-driven educational videos for an Instagram and YouTube Shorts channel.

### Target audience

- Indian students in CBSE Classes 6–10.
- Teenagers aged approximately 11–16.
- Parents and grandparents.
- Teachers and general knowledge enthusiasts.

### Content categories

Rotate between these categories:

1. Mathematics: arithmetic, algebra, geometry, percentages, probability, number patterns, tricks and common mistakes.
2. Science: physics, chemistry, biology, everyday mysteries and scientific explanations.
3. English: grammar, vocabulary, punctuation, pronunciation and interesting language facts.
4. Hindi: grammar, मुहावरे, पर्यायवाची, विलोम शब्द, शब्दों की उत्पत्ति and interesting language facts.
5. Social Science: history, geography, civics, economics and Indian Constitution.
6. General Knowledge: India, world, inventions, discoveries, environment and space.
7. Teenage psychology: emotions, confidence, self-esteem, motivation and decision-making.
8. Friendship: peer pressure, bullying, boundaries, trust and healthy friendships.
9. Moral education: honesty, kindness, empathy, responsibility, respect and discipline.
10. Parenting: communication between parents and teenagers, understanding differences and avoiding unnecessary conflict.
11. Grandparents and family: relationships, family stories, respect and intergenerational learning.
12. Study improvement: concentration, memory, revision, exam preparation and learning techniques.
13. Time management: procrastination, planning, daily routines and balancing study with recreation.
14. Life skills: communication, financial literacy, digital safety, problem-solving and critical thinking.

### Script requirements

- Each script should have 50–70 narration words.
- Duration should be approximately 20–30 seconds.
- Start immediately with a specific question or surprising statement.
- Never start with generic phrases such as "Did you know?".
- Build curiosity naturally.
- Explain one central idea only.
- Use simple conversational English.
- Use relatable Indian examples where appropriate.
- Give a satisfying answer near the end.
- End with a memorable educational takeaway.
- Do not use filler, forced suspense or exaggerated claims.
- Do not add calls to action.
- Write original scripts, never copy another creator.

### Research requirements

Research at least five possible topics before selecting the strongest.

For factual subjects:
- Verify central claims using reliable sources.
- Prefer NCERT, CBSE, universities, government agencies, museums, peer-reviewed research and established scientific organisations.
- Cross-check important facts.
- Provide source links.
- Never invent statistics, quotations or scientific explanations.

For CBSE curriculum topics:
- Identify the relevant class and subject.
- Keep the explanation consistent with the appropriate school-level concept.
- Verify formulas and worked examples.
- Explain the concept accurately, not just as a shortcut.

For psychology and parenting:
- Distinguish established findings from general advice.
- Avoid diagnosing teenagers.
- Avoid making absolute claims about human behaviour.
- Use balanced and empathetic language.

### Animation requirements

The video will use simple 2D animation.

For each script, also provide:
1. A scene-by-scene visual plan.
2. Simple character and object suggestions.
3. On-screen text for important words or calculations.
4. Suggested transitions.
5. A visual payoff at the end.

Avoid complicated animation, realistic 3D rendering and too many characters.

### Output format

For each video provide:

1. Video number.
2. Category.
3. Target CBSE class, where relevant.
4. One-sentence idea description.
5. Curiosity-based title.
6. Complete voiceover.
7. Exact narration word count.
8. Estimated duration.
9. Scene-by-scene 2D animation plan.
10. Caption for Instagram.
11. Three to five relevant hashtags.
12. Reliable research sources.

### Originality rule

Check the previous topics and scripts supplied in the conversation.

Never repeat an existing topic or create a slight variation of it.

Vary categories between successive videos.

### Batch generation

Generate five complete video concepts per request.

Prioritise quality, accuracy, relatability and audience interest over quantity.

# PART 3 — CONTENT STRATEGY FOR WIDER REACH

## 1. Build a recognisable channel identity

Avoid making the page look like a random collection of educational facts.

Use a consistent identity such as:

**Possible channel positioning:**

"Smart Minds – Learn Something New Every Day"

Alternative positioning:
- The Curious Classroom
- Beyond the Textbook
- Smart Student Club
- Brain Boost India
- 30 Seconds of Learning
- Little Lessons, Big Ideas

The channel should feel like a combination of a fun science show, a smart friend and a helpful teacher.

## 2. Use six recurring video series

Create recurring series so viewers know what to expect.

| Series | Content |
|---|---|
| Maths Without Fear | Maths concepts and shortcuts |
| Science Around Us | Everyday science mysteries |
| Teen Talk | Friendship and teenage psychology |
| Smart Student Secrets | Study techniques and exams |
| India Explained | History, geography and GK |
| Good Habits, Great Lives | Moral values and family relationships |

Give each series its own accent colour, but keep the overall animation style consistent.

## 3. Suggested weekly posting schedule

Start with five Reels per week. Consistency and quality are more important than publishing many weak videos.

| Day | Video category | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Maths | Why is zero special? |
| Tuesday | Science | Why does ice float? |
| Wednesday | Teen psychology | How to say no to peer pressure |
| Thursday | English / GK | Why is queue spelled like that? |
| Friday | Study improvement | Why does self-testing work? |
| Saturday | Optional | Parent-teen relationship or family values |
| Sunday | Stories / interaction | Quiz, poll or audience question |

## 4. Improve the first three seconds

The opening question determines whether someone continues watching.

Weak opening:
"Hello friends, today we are going to learn about percentages."

Better opening:
"Can you calculate 15% of 200 without a calculator?"

Weak opening:
"Today we will discuss friendship."

Better opening:
"Would you lose your values just to impress your friends?"

Weak opening:
"Let's understand the water cycle."

Better opening:
"How can the same water you drink have existed millions of years ago?"

Keep the opening question specific, understandable and visually interesting.

## 5. Make the animation memorable

Use a consistent visual language.

Recommended approach:

- Vertical 9:16 video.
- Simple, colourful 2D cartoon characters.
- Indian school settings where relevant.
- Large, readable subtitles.
- Highlight important numbers and keywords.
- Use arrows, diagrams and moving objects.
- Use simple background music at low volume.
- Use a consistent narrator voice.
- Avoid crowded backgrounds.

Develop two or three recurring characters:

**Character A: Curious student**

Asks interesting questions and represents the audience.

**Character B: Smart friend**

Explains concepts with simple examples.

**Character C: Teacher or mentor**

Provides a short clarification or practical takeaway when needed.

The characters can appear across mathematics, science and life lessons. This creates familiarity.

## 6. Improve views through shareable educational content

Make some videos useful enough that students want to share them with classmates.

Examples:

- "Send this to a friend who hates mathematics."
- "The percentage trick every student should know."
- "One mistake students make in algebra."
- "How to refuse peer pressure without losing your friends."
- "A simple way to remember the difference between speed and velocity."

Avoid adding generic calls to action to every narration. Put optional sharing invitations in captions when appropriate.

Focus on three outcomes:

1. **Watch:** People stay until the explanation is complete.
2. **Save:** Students save useful study techniques and formulas.
3. **Share:** Teenagers send relatable videos to friends and family.

## 7. Use captions that add value

Do not simply repeat the entire voiceover.

Example:

**Reel topic:** Why does minus multiplied by minus become plus?

Caption:

"Maths is not about memorising rules. It is about understanding patterns.

Save this explanation for your next revision session.

#CBSEMaths #Class10Maths #MathsMadeEasy #IndianStudents #StudyTips"

Use a small set of highly relevant hashtags rather than filling captions with unrelated trending tags.

Include natural search terms in captions, such as:
- CBSE Class 10 Mathematics
- Class 9 Science
- Study tips for students
- Teenage motivation
- English grammar
- Indian history facts

## 8. Use Instagram Stories to build a community

Stories can turn passive viewers into regular followers.

Suggested activities:

**Monday:** One mathematics quiz.

**Tuesday:** Science true-or-false question.

**Wednesday:** Friendship situation with two possible choices.

**Thursday:** English vocabulary quiz.

**Friday:** Ask students what subject they find difficult.

**Weekend:** Share answers and respond to selected questions.

Use question stickers, polls and quizzes to learn what the audience wants.

## 9. Collaborate to reach new audiences

Potential collaboration partners:

- CBSE teachers.
- Mathematics tutors.
- Science educators.
- School counsellors.
- Parenting educators.
- English-language teachers.
- Student communities.
- Educational Instagram pages.

Try collaborative Reels where a teacher explains a concept and your animated characters illustrate it.

Always add original value rather than reposting someone else's video.

## 10. Publish in multiple formats

One research topic can become several pieces of content.

Example: Peer pressure.

- Reel: Why do teenagers follow their friends?
- Carousel: Five ways to handle peer pressure.
- Story: What would you do in this situation?
- Quiz: Healthy friendship or unhealthy friendship?
- Longer video: How parents can talk to teenagers about peer pressure.

This increases the value of each research session.

## 11. Track performance instead of guessing

Maintain a spreadsheet of every published video.

Record:

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Views / reach | Audience discovery |
| Average watch time | How long viewers stay |
| Completion rate | Whether the video holds attention |
| Shares / sends | Whether viewers recommend it |
| Saves | Whether the content is useful |
| Comments | Whether it creates discussion |
| Profile visits | Whether viewers want more |
| Follows | Whether the content converts viewers into followers |

After every 10 videos, identify:

- Which category attracted the most viewers?
- Which hooks worked best?
- Which videos received the most shares?
- Which topics were saved most often?
- At what point did viewers stop watching?

Create more content based on actual results.

Instagram's Professional Dashboard includes a Best Practices section with guidance about creation, engagement, reach and account-specific performance.

# PART 5 — QUALITY CONTROL BEFORE PUBLISHING

Every video must pass these checks.

- [ ] Does the first sentence create genuine curiosity?
- [ ] Is the topic different from previous videos?
- [ ] Is the explanation factually accurate?
- [ ] Have important facts been cross-checked?
- [ ] Is the narration between 50 and 70 words?
- [ ] Can a Class 7 student understand it?
- [ ] Does the video have one central message?
- [ ] Are the examples relevant to Indian students?
- [ ] Are all on-screen calculations correct?
- [ ] Are the animations simple and readable?
- [ ] Are subtitles clear on a mobile screen?
- [ ] Is the voiceover natural?
- [ ] Are sources recorded for factual claims?
- [ ] Does the video avoid misleading or exaggerated claims?
- [ ] Does the final takeaway add value?

For science, mathematics and health-related topics, accuracy must come before entertainment.

For psychology, parenting and moral education, avoid shaming students or blaming parents. Present practical, balanced advice.

# PART 6 — RECOMMENDED 90-DAY GROWTH PLAN

## Month 1: Establish the identity

- Finalise channel name and logo.
- Create two recurring animated characters.
- Publish approximately five videos per week.
- Test all six main content series.
- Build a consistent thumbnail and subtitle style.
- Record which topics attract the strongest response.

## Month 2: Find the winning topics

- Study the first month's performance.
- Increase the share of videos from successful categories.
- Experiment with different opening questions.
- Introduce weekly quizzes.
- Publish useful educational carousels.
- Try collaborations with educators.

## Month 3: Build a recognisable educational brand

- Create recurring themed series.
- Make more videos based on audience questions.
- Repurpose suitable content for YouTube Shorts.
- Build a library of revision videos.
- Collaborate with teachers and educational creators.
- Review follower growth, watch time and shares.

Do not judge success only by follower count. A small audience of students who regularly watch, save and share your educational videos is a valuable foundation.

# FINAL RECOMMENDATION

Build the channel around one central promise:

**"Every day, learn something interesting that makes you smarter, more confident and better prepared for life."**

Do not make the channel feel like an online tuition class.

Make it feel like a place where a teenager can discover something interesting in 30 seconds, learn a useful school concept, understand people better and develop good habits.

Use Claude for research and scripts, a consistent 2D animation workflow for production, and Instagram Insights to decide what to create next.

**The most important rule: Make every video worth watching, worth remembering and worth sharing.**
